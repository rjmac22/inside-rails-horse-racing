# Great Britain Study 01 — Governance and Structure

## Question

How is British racing organised at the level needed to describe the observed national racing programme coherently?

## Executive conclusion

British racing is an almost-daily national programme assembled from relatively standard-sized individual cards. A typical analytical course-date meeting contains seven races; a typical national racing day contains about four such meetings and 28 races. The programme is systematically larger on Saturdays and through late spring and summer, while 2020 is a clear exception to the otherwise stable observed structure.

## Evidence base

The study analysed **111,634 Great Britain races** from **1 January 2015 through 27 May 2026**. For this study only, races sharing the same source date and analytical course label were grouped as a **course-date meeting**. That grouping was explicitly analytical and was not claimed to be the official BHA definition of a meeting or fixture.

Across the study population:

- **15,865** course-date meetings were observed;
- **7 races** was both the most common and median meeting size;
- **94.8%** of meetings contained six to eight races;
- **4,016** British racing days were observed;
- the median racing day contained **4 meetings and 28 races**;
- Saturday had the largest programme, with a median **5 meetings and 38 races** in complete non-2020 years;
- Sunday was materially smaller, with a median **2 meetings and 14 races**;
- racing still occurred on at least **97.5% of calendar dates for every weekday** in the complete non-2020 comparison years;
- the programme expanded strongly into spring and summer;
- **May was the busiest month by mean races per racing day** in every complete non-2020 year from 2015 to 2025.

## What this means

The British programme has a stable core scale but a strong calendar rhythm. Seven races is a useful reference point for an ordinary card, while a national day of roughly four meetings and 28 races is a useful overall benchmark. Saturdays and spring/summer dates generally carry materially more racing.

These are empirical patterns, not regulatory rules, and the study does not establish why racing is scheduled this way.

## Important limits

- The course-date meeting was a study grouping, not an official BHA entity.
- **2020** was structurally unusual and was not used as a normal-year baseline.
- **2026** is partial through 27 May.
- The study describes programme organisation and scale, not commercial importance or causation.
- Racecourse identity was deliberately deferred to Study 03.
