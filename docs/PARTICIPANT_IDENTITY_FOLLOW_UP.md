# Participant Identity Follow-Up

## Purpose

This note preserves mandatory follow-up work from Notebook 20 for the later participant identity studies.

Notebook 20 closed source-field semantics, blank governance and bounded supplementation for `jockey`, `trainer` and `owner`. It did not establish canonical participant identities, and 18 reviewed blank connection fields remain unresolved:

- 5 trainer fields;
- 13 owner fields;
- 0 jockey fields.

These unresolved fields do not invalidate the affected runner or race records. They remain governed nulls rather than guessed values. Across 1,851,285 runner rows they are immaterial to broad population totals, but each could affect the retrospective record of a particular trainer or owner by one runner-level appearance.

## Notebook 22 — Jockey and trainer identity

Notebook 22 must reopen the five unresolved trainer fields as a targeted authority-contact queue.

For each record:

1. retain the existing Notebook 20 verification ID and exact `(source_rowid, source_field)` target;
2. assemble the exact date, course, off time, race name, horse and jurisdiction;
3. exhaust available official and established historical result archives;
4. identify the responsible racing authority, racecourse archive or official results/licensing department;
5. send a concise enquiry asking for the officially recorded trainer;
6. preserve the enquiry, recipient, sent date, response, evidence locator and confidence;
7. update the governed repair only where the reply or documentary evidence is specific and defensible;
8. retain the field as unresolved where no answer is received or evidence conflicts.

The notebook must report how any unresolved trainer affects identity-aware career totals and must not silently exclude it from completeness statements.

## Notebook 23 — Owner identity and ownership structures

Notebook 23 must reopen the 13 unresolved owner fields through the same authority-contact process, adapted for ownership records.

Possible evidence sources include:

- the racing authority responsible for the race;
- official historical-results or registration departments;
- racecourse archives;
- national studbook or racing-calendar records;
- established archived racecards or results providers.

Owner evidence must distinguish a person from a partnership, syndicate, club, company or other ownership entity. A reply containing a display label is not sufficient by itself to justify splitting or merging ownership entities.

## Correspondence standard

Every enquiry should include only the information needed to identify the record:

- race date;
- course;
- scheduled or recorded off time;
- race name;
- horse;
- missing field;
- source jurisdiction;
- a request for the officially recorded value.

All correspondence used as evidence must be captured with provenance in the governed manual-verification system. Private contact details should not be committed unnecessarily; retain only the institutional recipient, date, evidence summary and durable locator permitted by the source.

## Stopping rule

A blank may be supplemented only when evidence identifies the exact missing field for the exact runner record. Non-response, ambiguous replies and conflicting evidence remain governed nulls.

Database completeness does not require inventing a value. Completeness requires that the missingness, investigation, evidence and unresolved status are represented accurately and remain visible to downstream analysis.
